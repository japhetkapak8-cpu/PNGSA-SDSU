PNG STUDENT GUIDE

Banking & Phone Setup

A practical starting guide for Papua New Guinean students setting up banking and mobile phone services after arriving in the United States.

Before You Begin

Requirements vary between banks and mobile providers. Always confirm current requirements directly with the institution before opening an account or purchasing a plan.

Banking Setup Phone Setup Security Tips Arrival Checklist
PART 1

Setting Up a U.S. Bank Account

A checking account can make it easier to receive money, pay bills, use a debit card and manage everyday expenses.

1

Gather Your Documents

A bank may ask for several forms of identification. Requirements differ, so ask the bank what it accepts.

Passport
Student ID
U.S. address
Immigration documents if requested
U.S. phone number if available
Initial deposit if required
2

Choose the Right Account

Most students begin with a checking account. Compare fees and requirements before choosing one.

Monthly Fees

Look for student accounts or accounts without monthly maintenance fees.

Debit Card

Confirm that the account includes a debit card and understand any replacement fees.

ATM Access

Check whether there are convenient ATMs near campus or your housing.

Mobile Banking

Look for online banking, mobile deposits, transaction alerts and account controls.

3

Understand Your Account Numbers

Your checking account will typically have two important numbers.

Routing Number

Identifies the U.S. financial institution.

Account Number

Identifies your individual bank account.

Do not publicly share your bank account number, PIN, password or verification codes.

4

Set Up Online Banking

Once your account is active, configure your banking app and security settings.

  • Create a strong and unique password.
  • Enable multi-factor authentication when available.
  • Turn on transaction and balance alerts.
  • Review transactions regularly.
  • Never give verification codes to someone who calls you.
PART 2

Setting Up a U.S. Phone

A U.S. phone number is useful for banking, school accounts, emergency communication and everyday services.

1

Check Your Current Phone

Before purchasing another phone, check whether your current device can work with a U.S. provider.

Phone is unlocked
Supports U.S. network bands
Supports SIM or eSIM
Battery and device are in good condition
2

Prepaid or Postpaid?

SIMPLE OPTION

Prepaid

You pay before using the service. This may be easier for students who are newly arriving in the United States.

  • Usually simpler to begin
  • Helps control monthly spending
  • No long-term commitment on many plans
ANOTHER OPTION

Postpaid

Service is billed after use and may include additional eligibility or identification requirements.

  • Monthly billing
  • Family/group plans may be available
  • Requirements can vary by provider
3

Compare Plans

Don't look only at the advertised monthly price. Compare what the plan actually includes.

Coverage

Make sure service works well around campus, housing and places you regularly travel.

Mobile Data

Check monthly data limits and what happens when the limit is reached.

International Calling

Check the cost of calls or messages to Papua New Guinea if you need them.

Total Cost

Review activation, taxes, phone financing and other possible charges.

4

Protect Your Phone Number

Your phone number may become connected to banking, university accounts and identity verification.

  • Protect your mobile provider account with a password or PIN.
  • Don't share one-time verification codes.
  • Keep your recovery email updated.
  • Enable device screen-lock security.
  • Report a lost or stolen phone quickly.

Protect Yourself From Scams

New students can be targeted by scams involving banking, immigration, taxes, employment and phone accounts.

Never Share Passwords

PNGSA, your university and legitimate financial institutions should not need your account password.

Protect Verification Codes

Treat one-time codes like passwords. Do not send them to another person.

Be Careful With Urgent Calls

Be suspicious of callers demanding immediate payment or threatening arrest, deportation or account closure.

Verify Links

Avoid signing into important accounts through unexpected text-message or email links.
